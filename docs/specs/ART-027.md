## Why this exists
Lucid's verdict on G0: "the graphics look absolute dogshit still". That was expected: G0 is flat-coloured tiles under a procedural sky. G1 is where overgrown starts to look like **Lucid's paintings** (`docs/art/moodboard/`). Lucid wants an artist with real freedom who doesn't ask questions and who uses everything it can do.

## The brief: what you are free to do
You are the art director and the technical artist. Nobody prescribes your technique. Things you might reach for, none of them required:
- screen-space painterly filters (Kuwahara and anisotropic Kuwahara, brush-stroke or flow-field passes, impasto normal tricks);
- palette and colour grading (LUTs, ramps, tone curves);
- canvas or paper grain;
- edge treatment;
- stylised terrain materials (triplanar, painted splat textures, stroke textures, noise);
- a sky shader (cosmic eyes, spirals, nebulae, galaxies, stars, layered clouds);
- volumetric fog and god rays, glow, light shafts;
- grass and foliage (MultiMesh, shader grass, billboards, cards);
- trees and rocks: Blender-scripted, procedural meshes in GDScript, or D8-allowed CC0 bases reworked into the style;
- wind shaders, water shaders, time of day.

Use any reference, technique or source you like. If a mood-board image could ship as a backdrop, skybox layer or texture source, D8 allows that too (the images are Lucid's own).

**Exhaust your ability.** Don't stop at "acceptable". Push until captures set beside the mood board would make Lucid say "that's the game".

## The frame (hard constraints)
1. **Your north star** is the mood board: its `README.md` style traits and the 13 images. Look at them yourself with the Read tool, and often.
   - The painted (oil, impasto) look.
   - Teal and blue grounds against burnt orange and gold, very dark darks, glowing lights.
   - The sky as a character: cosmic eyes and spirals.
   - Wind everywhere.
   - Small figure, huge world.
2. **It's the real game.** Work in `game/` on this branch, on the actual streamed world, the tilted camera and the wanderer.
   - **Never change rules code** (`game/src/rules/**`, `game/src/world/**` except new render-only helpers, `contracts/**`). The world's tiles, biomes and heights are a frozen contract.
   - Views read rules state and never write it.
   - Seeded visual placement (grass, trees, rocks) must be deterministic: derive it from `OgRng.derive_seed(seed, cx, cy, …)`, never `randf`.
3. **Gameplay stays readable** with the post stack on, a G1 acceptance criterion: the wanderer, cliffs, water, the cursor and the attack swing must read clearly in play view. Make a capture that shows it.
4. **Performance budget:** Lucid's machine is an AMD Strix Halo (Radeon 8060S iGPU), with a target of 60 fps at 1080p–1440p.
   - The cloud renders in software, so you can't measure fps. Stay sane anyway: full-screen passes kept modest, instanced foliage with LOD or distance fade, no per-pixel loops over large kernels at full resolution without a downsample.
   - Put a quality toggle for the post stack in the code (one constant, or a `--post off|low|high` flag) so Lucid can compare.
   - In your report, write down the GPU cost you expect for each pass.
5. **Assets: D8** (`CLAUDE.md` hard rules). Anything you make is fine. Third-party files go only under `game/assets/third_party/` with a CREDITS row, and `bash tools/check-credits.sh` must pass. No ripped or unknown-licence assets.
6. **Keep these working:**
   - `bash tools/godot/test.sh` → all pass (add tests only for real breakage, for example deterministic foliage placement);
   - `bash tools/godot/export.sh` → both builds;
   - the CI smoke capture.
7. **Don't touch:**
   - `character_view.gd`'s character body: ART-028 replaces the wanderer. Adding a shadow or outline treatment for characters is fine, through a material or post pass.
   - `camera_rig.gd` and `local_input.gd`: GOD-008 changes them now.
   - If you need camera changes, describe them in the report instead.

## Deliverables
- **The look, in the game,** on this branch:
  - post stack;
  - sky;
  - terrain materials;
  - foliage and props for at least the Grassland and Forest biomes (others may stay simpler, but must not clash);
  - water;
  - wind;
  - one **landmark vista**: a seeded landmark that the vista camera (hold V) frames well.
- **`docs/art/BIBLE.md`:** the new art bible, which replaces the pixel-era `docs/art/DIRECTION.md` as the source of truth. It covers:
  - the principles and the palette (with values);
  - how each layer is built;
  - the rules future art follows (props, monsters, VFX) so they stay in style;
  - which mood-board traits each technique serves.
- **`docs/art/g1-lab/`:**
  - `PROGRESS.md`, a running log: what you tried, what you rejected and why;
  - captures, each at ≤ 1280×720. Include before-and-after pairs, and side-by-sides of each final capture next to the mood-board image it answers. Composite them with Godot's Image, or another tool you have.
- **The final set of captures:**
  - play view in Grassland, Forest and at a river;
  - the vista at the landmark;
  - a readability capture mid-attack;
  - post quality off, low and high.
- **Your final report:**
  - what you built, file by file;
  - the expected GPU cost per pass;
  - what you'd do next;
  - the 4–6 best captures as absolute paths, so the orchestrator can show Lucid.

## Working method
- **Commit and push often:** `git -C /home/user/wt/g1-world-lab push -q origin g1-world-lab`, retrying up to 4 times. The container can restart, and a lab that dies unpushed is lost.
- **Keep `PROGRESS.md` current** after every step, so a restarted session can resume from it.
- **Iterate in loops:** capture, compare against the mood board, critique honestly, change, repeat. Do at least 6 loops. Write each critique into `PROGRESS.md`.
- **Captures:** use `bash tools/godot/capture.sh <out.png> forward_plus [args]`, with `--seed`, `--at X,Z`, `--vista`, `--autopilot` and `--capture-tick N`. Run `bash tools/godot/test.sh` first after adding scripts (captures don't import). Software rendering is slow, at about 4 fps, so plan your capture budget.
- **Read the traps first:** read `CLAUDE.md`'s Project traps before starting, especially WorkerThreadPool, fog vs sky, the `.uid` files, and "build child nodes in `_init`".
