## Desired result
overgrown looks like a game people want to screenshot. It matches the GDD's vibe: saturated, cozy and lighthearted on the surface, with brutal depth underneath (see "The game" below). Characters read instantly at game scale, every species is unmistakable, and every biome has its own mood.

The work rests on two documents written in this PR:
- `docs/art/RESEARCH.md`: what the best open-source game-art and design knowledge says, with sources.
- `docs/art/DIRECTION.md`: overgrown's art bible, which every future art task (props, monsters, weapons, VFX, buildings, UI icons) builds on.

## The game (creative brief, from docs/gdd/00-overview.md; read it, and skim 10-art-audio.md)
- **What it is:** a top-down 3/4 real-time hack-and-slash sandbox RPG that runs in a browser tab, even on a phone. Worlds come from a seed and never end. You are not the chosen one, just one more inhabitant of a living world. NPCs and monsters live, learn, fight, breed, migrate and die by the same rules you do.
- **What it can become:** a world that tells stories without a writer. Monsters age, grey and scar. Abandoned villages are overrun. You find a corpse wearing someone else's gear. Biomes shift with seasons and weather. A level 40 bear sleeps in its den ten metres from a level 2 horned bunny. Players grow by doing: their gear, their scars and their fashion are their history. Multiplayer is a share link.
- **Vibe:** cozy at a glance, but the details give it away. Lighthearted, saturated and funny (a big mob knocks a tree onto your head, and the tree does the damage), over permadeath, corpse runs and dungeons that remember who they killed. Each biome has its own palette. Magic, hits and loot always *pop* (a global pop palette reserved for them).
- **References Lucid named:** Zelda top-down (3/4 readability), Stardew-style perspective, Valheim, Metin2, Cube World, Dwarf Fortress/Kenshi emergent stories.
- **Characters:** 9 species on one shared humanoid skeleton: human, elf, dwarf, orc, foxfolk, bearfolk, rabbitfolk, birdfolk, lizardfolk. Later they will wear layered armour, clothes, dyes and visible aging (paper doll, ART-010–012), so bodies must leave room for gear.

## Phases (in order; commit after each)

### Phase 1: Research (`docs/art/RESEARCH.md`)
Dig deep online; this is the foundation. Find and actually read:
- **Open-source skill and agent-instruction repositories** for game design, game art, pixel art and visual design (for example Claude/agent `SKILL.md` collections, design-system and art-direction skills, game-design-document templates). Find the best ones and say what makes them good.
- **Pixel art craft:**
  - top-down 3/4 character conventions at 32 px;
  - silhouette and readability at small scale;
  - hue-shifted colour ramps and palette design (Lospec-style);
  - selective outlining, cluster cleanup, anti-aliasing by hand, and avoiding pillow shading and banding;
  - animation principles at low frame counts (anticipation, smears, squash and stretch, sub-pixel motion);
  - top-down terrain tiles and dual-grid / autotile transitions without visible repetition.
- **Procedural pixel-art generation:** open-source generators (sprite, character, terrain), how they get good results from code, and what makes procedural art look hand-made rather than generated.
- **Games with the target vibe** (cozy-saturated top-down): what their palettes, shapes and lighting do. Analyse in words only.

For every source give a link and its licence when it is code. End the file with **"What we take"**: concrete, numbered rules we'll apply.

### Phase 2: Art bible (`docs/art/DIRECTION.md`)
Your creative call, grounded in Phase 1. Cover at least:
- palette philosophy and ramps (the biome palettes, actor palette and pop palette, and how they relate);
- value structure, i.e. what must stand out from what;
- outline rules;
- light direction;
- shape language per species and per biome;
- proportions on the 32 px cell;
- animation principles for our frame counts;
- tile rules: variation, transitions, avoiding grid-feel;
- a short "don't" list;
- a section for future art tasks (props, monsters, weapons, VFX) so they inherit the style.

### Phase 3: Redraw
Rework the generators, palettes, species table, skeleton proportions and poses to match the bible. Everything that's drawn is yours to change: shapes, shading, outlines, colours, features, poses and timing within the locked frame structure below.

Iterate visually. Render preview sheets, upscale them with nearest neighbour (×4 to ×8), **look at them with the Read tool** and critique them against the bible. Then redraw, and keep going until you'd be proud to show it. Also judge the art at **real game scale**:
- `npm run build`;
- `npm -w @overgrown/client run preview -- --port 4211 --strictPort`;
- `node scripts/check-page.mjs http://localhost:4211/?seed=N shot.png 3000`, for a few seeds that show different biomes.

Preview scripts live in the scratchpad, not the repo.

### Phase 4: Verify and report
Run the verification commands below. Write before/after preview sheets (actors: every species × direction for idle, walk and attack; tiles: every biome palette with transitions) and in-game screenshots to `/home/user/overgrown-art-previews/` (outside the repo) and list them in the report.

## Locked (the game depends on these; everything else is yours)
- **Everything is ours (hard rule).**
  - Every pixel is drawn by our Python code.
  - No downloaded, traced or AI-generated images, and no image files committed.
  - Learn techniques from anything, but write our own code. If an algorithm is closely adapted from a source, credit it in a comment and in RESEARCH.md; it must come from a permissively licensed or public-domain source.
  - Don't add dependencies.
- **Determinism:** same code gives byte-identical output. Randomness only through `lib/rng.py` seeded helpers. No `random`, no time, no hash() of str; `PYTHONHASHSEED`-independent.
- **Cell sizes:** actors 32×32, tiles 16×16 (GDD visual spec; the camera, collision box and dual-grid ground layer assume them).
- **Frame names and structure:**
  - Names: `actor/<species>/<anim>/<dir>/<i>` and `tile/<palette>/<terrain>/<mask>/<variant>`.
  - Fixed: the species keys, anim names, dirs, the right-mirrors-left rule, the tile mask bits, the terrains, the Biome→palette map and terrain precedence.
  - You MAY change the per-anim frame counts in `contracts/actor-anims.json` and the variant counts in `contracts/tile-frames.json`. If you do, update their TS twins (`client/src/render/actor-anims.ts` and its `ticksPerFrame`/timing, `client/src/render/tile-frames.ts`) and keep both test suites green.
- **Skeleton contract:**
  - Keep the joint names, the l/r convention, `SOLE_Y` meaning the sole line, `hand_anchor`, and "`tail_root` never moves".
  - Joint positions and proportions are yours. The feet must stay on the sole line: the sim's foot point is the sprite's anchor.
- **Palette file format:** `lib/palette.py`'s schema and validation stay. Colours, ramp lengths within the schema, and the palette files' contents are yours. The pop palette stays reserved for magic, hits, loot and UI.
- **Atlas:** everything stays in the single `core` atlas at most 2048×2048. Bytes are startup time (M1 bar: first frame ≤ 3 s on slow 4G), so report the atlas PNG size before and after. More than +25% needs a stated reason in the report.

## Acceptance criteria
- AC1 Research is real: `docs/art/RESEARCH.md` cites ≥ 15 distinct sources, each with a URL, including ≥ 3 open-source skill or agent-instruction repositories you read, and ends with "What we take". Proven by: `grep -c "https://" docs/art/RESEARCH.md` ≥ 15, plus the report naming the 3 best repos and why.
- AC2 The bible exists and the art follows it: `docs/art/DIRECTION.md` has the sections listed in Phase 2. The report maps ≥ 8 bible rules to the code that implements them (file:function).
- AC3 Before/after preview sheets and ≥ 3 in-game screenshots (different biomes, character visible) exist in `/home/user/overgrown-art-previews/`. The report has one honest line per image on what improved and what still bothers you.
- AC4 Determinism holds: the build-twice block in Verification prints `BYTE-IDENTICAL`.
- AC5 Every contract check passes: `npm run check && npm test && npm run build` → exit 0. The game boots: `node scripts/check-page.mjs http://localhost:4211/ /tmp/art024.png 3000` → exit 0, `"errors": []`.
- AC6 Tests: tests that pinned the old look's incidental details may be changed. Tests that guard real breakage stay or get stronger: fit in the cell, feet on the sole line, mirror rule, palette validation, distinct biome palettes, determinism. The report lists every test changed or removed and why.
