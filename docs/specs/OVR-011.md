## Lucid's idea, verbatim (for the record; quote the key lines in the OQ rows)
> "imagine those bullet heaven games where every level up gives like an upgrade to pick from three choices which eventually can scale to absurd strengths … you gather exp you level up, you get those choices from 3 things, and you gather your equipment and whatnot and ability books … you play your character between dungeons and overworld, the dungeons you cant die, if u dead you get some penalty … its those bullet heavens in the over world, but more like the further you go outside the more of a monster horde nonsense it is, and if you die, well you have your gravestone there with your equipment but your character is dead so you gotta make a new one, we need some meta progression as a reason to push outwards"

Answers to the follow-up questions:
- **Combat:** "hybrid but the combat should still be like hack and slash horde slayer, the 'bullet heaven' was more like to explain you my vision, we can make SOME skills autofire but we have mana, so toggles, but maybe theres passives that make this easier or something, there should be real buildable OP SHIT but also pay offs"
- **Level-ups:** "Books feed the draft but also level ups increase passively the base attack or something (think traits, abilities and skills)"
- **Meta progression:** "The world remembers, Knowledge codex, Heirlooms / grave". No account unlocks: "if anything the 'classic roguelite meta' is the other three points, so if my npcs [sic; read as "my character", and see the NPC note below] dies somewhere, that landmark serves as a civilization point, this would counter the enemies being able to erase/take over villages or cities, so its a proper back and forth based on skill and prep, (needs balancing probably) … like a tug of war kinda thingy"
- **Overworld death:** "Always permadeath"

## Locked decisions (new OQ rows OQ-040 to OQ-044, dated 2026-10-20, status resolved)
1. **OQ-040: Combat shape (the horde slayer).**
   - Combat is a real-time hack-and-slash horde slayer: the player aims, swings and dodges by hand (the G0 controls).
   - **Some skills can be toggled to auto-fire**, paying mana while on. Passives can make toggles cheaper or easier, for example via mana regeneration or cost reduction.
   - Builds can become absurdly strong ("real buildable OP"), and strong builds carry trade-offs ("pay offs").
   - Hordes are the normal encounter far from the origin.
2. **OQ-041: Level-up draft.**
   - Each level-up offers **3 choices**, and the player picks one.
   - **What the choices are drawn from:** a pool fed by what the character has: Ability Books read, gear carried, teachings learned (learn or rank up an ability or technique). Generic boons are also in the pool.
   - **Base power:** every level-up also raises base power passively ("base attack or something").
   - **Traits still grow by use** (04-characters, unchanged).
   - **Level points (1 per level, spent on Ability Books) are replaced by the draft.** An Ability Book now adds its ability to the draft pool.
   - **Open, decided when the draft task is specced:** draft size per rarity, rerolls, the generic boon list, and how much base power one level adds. Mark them "decided in the draft task" and invent no numbers.
3. **OQ-042: Overworld death is always permadeath.**
   - **World modes:** the per-world "Respawn" mode is removed. Permadeath is the rule in every world. "Hardcore" (solo; the world is deleted on death) stays as an optional host setting.
   - **What stays:** the downed state and ally revive (OQ-012), the grave or corpse with the full inventory, and dungeon death (teleport out plus XP loss that can de-level, raising the dungeon's difficulty).
   - **Supersedes** OQ-011's "Respawn" option.
3b. **Dungeons as the safe-ish loop:** the play rhythm is the overworld frontier (permadeath, hordes) ↔ dungeons (no permadeath, XP-loss penalty). This adds no new rule; record it as the intended loop in 00-overview.
4. **OQ-043: The frontier: horde density by distance.**
   - Monster **density and horde size grow with distance from the world origin**. This adds to the existing creature-level distribution by distance (WLD-007, 02-world).
   - Near the origin it's calm; far out it's "monster horde nonsense".
   - Biomes stay about place, not difficulty (02-world).
5. **OQ-044: Meta progression is the world, not an account.** There are no account or currency unlocks. Three parts:
   - **The world remembers.** What a character changes stays: lit beacons or waystones at the frontier stay lit, and cleared dens and outposts stay changed. The next character benefits, for example by starting further out.
   - **Knowledge codex.** Ability Books and teachings a character learned are recorded. New characters relearn them cheaper or start with some.
     - **Default scope:** per world save.
     - **Decided in its task:** the exact benefit, and whether the codex follows the player profile across worlds.
   - **Heirlooms and the grave.**
     - The grave holds the dead character's gear (any character can loot it; the existing corpse rules).
     - One heirloom (or a bit of power) passes to the next character.
     - The grave becomes a **landmark that acts as a civilization point**.
   - **The tug of war.** Graves, lit beacons, villages and cities are **civilization anchors**: they push back against monsters spreading and taking over settlements (03-ecology, 06-npcs). Monsters push civilization back where nobody holds the line. It's a back and forth decided by skill and preparation.
     - Balancing comes later. Record it as a design goal plus tasks, with no numbers.
     - **"You are just another one" (pillar 1)** applies: by one rule set, notable NPC deaths can leave graves that anchor civilization too. Record that as an open point for the task, not a decision.
