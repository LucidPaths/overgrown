# ART-025 research notes

What I looked at and what I took from each. Sources are linked where they can be checked. Some entries are working
knowledge of well-known games and techniques. I say so where that is the case.

| Source | What I took |
|---|---|
| Nintendo, Iwata Asks: *A Link Between Worlds*, "Beautiful Teamwork" ([nintendo.co.uk](https://nintendo.co.uk/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-A-Link-Between-Worlds/The-Legend-of-Zelda-A-Link-Between-Worlds/4-Beautiful-Teamwork/4-Beautiful-Teamwork-832029.html)); Game Informer summary ([gameinformer.com](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2013/11/20/the-trick-behind-the-legend-of-zelda-a-link-between-worlds-top-down-view)); Siliconera ([siliconera.com](https://siliconera.com/?p=351033)) | The decisive trick. From a steep top-down camera you see hats, not faces, so ALBW leans characters and objects back toward the camera. I did it in two parts: a screen-space **lean** for all upright things (depth stays upright, so occlusion is honest) and an 18 deg **tilt** for characters. Before and after: `img/explore-no-lean-no-tilt.png` versus `img/species-lineup.png`. |
| t3ssel8r's 3D pixel-art work (YouTube channel [@t3ssel8r](https://www.youtube.com/@t3ssel8r); working knowledge, not re-verified this session) | The lineage of the whole direction: real 3D drawn at a low resolution, an orthographic camera snapped to texels with the sub-texel remainder applied at upscale, outlines from depth and normal discontinuities, and toon light with a few hard steps. |
| "Drawing the Line on 3D Pixel Art", Pixel Perfect series (transcript: [sozai.app](https://sozai.app/transcript/drawing-the-line-on-3d-pixel-art-pixel-perfect-transcript/index.md)) | Outlines must come from geometry (depth and normals), not colour: shadows and lighting changes create false colour edges. Hence `POST_FS` reads only depth, normal and material id. |
| "Developing 3D Pixel Art Rendering", UPC thesis ([upcommons.upc.edu](https://upcommons.upc.edu/handle/2117/416925)) | Confirms the common building blocks (low-res target, integer upscale, depth/normal edge detection) and that public documentation is thin. I wrote my own version from first principles. |
| Pixel-perfect camera guidance ([bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-debug-pixel-perfect-2d-rendering-issues)) | Shimmer comes from fractional camera positions, non-integer scales and filtering. Render offscreen at art resolution, upscale with point sampling by an integer, letterbox the rest. The blit pass in `renderer.ts` does this. |
| 80.lv, "5 ways to set up outlines in games" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/guide-5-ways-to-set-up-outlines-in-games/?amp=1)) | A survey of outline methods: inverted hull, fresnel, post-process edges. Post-process won because it gives 1-texel lines at any distance and costs one pass at the low resolution. |
| Ordered (Bayer) dithering (working knowledge; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_dithering)) | A 4x4 Bayer threshold used three ways: material boundaries (world-anchored), stepped light falloff, and alpha-free fading of particles, silhouettes and the slash ribbon. |
| *Hyper Light Drifter* (working knowledge) | A 480x270 internal resolution makes fights readable at a glance. I chose about 440 texels on the long axis for the same reason: a hack-and-slash needs a close camera. |
| *A Short Hike*, *Eastward* (working knowledge) | Warmth: tinted shadows, friendly chunky shapes and generous light. The pixel grid is a texture, not a constraint on the camera. |
| *Octopath Traveler*'s HD-2D (working knowledge) | The counterexample I kept in mind. Depth of field and bloom over pixel sprites look rich but blur the grid. I refused any post effect that breaks the pixel. |
| Classic SNES/GBA RPG character sprites (working knowledge) | Faces at this size are two dark pixels on a clear skin area, and big heads (1:2 to 1:2.5) carry identity. That produced the eye placement and hair-cap rules. |
| The repo's own previous attempt (`docs/art/previews/ingame-meadow-forest.png`, judged a failure; I did not study it beyond that) | A cautionary example: flat tile fields with a uniform speckle, stair-stepped biome borders, nothing standing on the ground, and a lone sprite. It reads as a map, not a place. That pushed me away from tiles and sprites, toward a world with height, objects, light and weather. |
